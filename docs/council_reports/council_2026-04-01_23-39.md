# Strategic Advisory Council Report
> Date: 2026-04-01 23:39
> Question: Kilter-Up — single product vs two-product split strategy

---

## Original Question

Should Kilter-Up split into two separate products (AI Coach + Climb Discovery) developed in parallel, or keep everything as one app with two major feature tracks?

- **Idea A — Kilter AI Coach**: Video upload → AI analysis with climb context. High friction, strong moat. €7.99/month.
- **Idea B — Kilter Climb Discovery**: Search 344k+ climbs, AI session builder. No video needed, low friction, broader audience.
- **Option 1**: Two separate products in parallel (risk: focus dilution)
- **Option 2**: One product, two tracks (Discovery funnels to Coach)

---

## Advisor Analyses

### 🔴 The Contrarian

The framing of "which product strategy" is a distraction from a more fundamental problem.

**Market ceiling.** Kilter Board ownership is the binding constraint. There are roughly 8,000-12,000 Kilter Boards worldwide per publicly available estimates. Even at 10% penetration with zero churn, that is 800-1,200 paying users maximum — €6,400-9,600 MRR ceiling. That does not support a sustainable bootstrapped SaaS. You are not building a niche product; you are building a product for a niche within a niche.

**Retention economics on Coach.** AI video analysis is episodic, not habitual. A climber films a session, gets feedback, improves over weeks. Then what? They churn because the product delivered on its promise. There is no daily or weekly forcing function. €7.99/month demands habitual use; episodic value delivery produces month-2 cancellations.

**Discovery does not solve this.** The funnel logic (Discovery feeds Coach) assumes Discovery users have Kilter Boards. But Discovery's "broader audience" is still Kilter Board users — the same ceiling applies. You are not expanding the addressable market; you are adding development surface area while the ceiling stays the same.

**Solo founder execution.** Two coherent products require two coherent roadmaps, two support queues, two feedback loops. The real risk is not focus dilution in the abstract — it is that neither product reaches the quality threshold required to convert skeptical early adopters before runway runs out.

**FATAL FLAW: The total addressable market (Kilter Board owners) is structurally too small to sustain bootstrapped SaaS economics regardless of which product strategy is chosen.**

---

### 💰 SaaS Monetization Expert

The two-product temptation is a classic solo-founder trap. You have one genuine moat: AI video analysis + Kilter Board context. Discovery is a feature, not a product — Climbdex already exists and it's free. Building a free competitor to a free tool is not a business.

**Why single product wins here:** Discovery's willingness-to-pay is near zero. Climbdex exists, it's open source, users already have it. You'd be charging for something people get free. Worse, it pulls your roadmap, support, and positioning in two directions simultaneously — fatal for a solo bootstrapper.

The Coach product has a real moat and a clear value metric: measurable technique improvement. That's what climbers pay for. V3-V7 climbers are exactly the segment that obsesses over progression and has disposable income for gear, gym memberships, and coaching. They are monetizable.

**Use Discovery as a conversion mechanism, not a product.** Offer basic climb search free inside the Coach app. Gated AI session building (warmups, circuits) becomes a Coach subscriber feature. This removes the "high friction only" objection — users get immediate value browsing climbs, then upgrade when they want the coaching loop.

€7.99/month is slightly under-anchored for a tool replacing a real coach. V3-V7 climbers pay €15-20/session for human coaching. You have room to go to €9.99/month or introduce an annual plan at €79/year (30% savings framing) to improve LTV and reduce churn.

**PRICING VERDICT: Single product, €9.99/month (or €79/year), Discovery as free in-app hook, Coach analysis as the paywall.**

### 🔵 First Principles Thinker

The framing assumes the core decision is *architecture* — single product vs two products. But that's a resource allocation question dressed up as a strategy question.

**Strip the assumptions:**
- "High friction" for video upload is treated as a liability. But friction is only bad if the value delivered doesn't justify it. A climber who uploads a video and gets a precise coaching cue for why they keep falling off the same move — that's transformative. Friction that precedes transformation is called *commitment*.
- Discovery is framed as lower friction, broader audience, easier entry. But Climbdex is free and open source. You cannot win on breadth against free. You can only win on depth.
- The "funnel" logic (Discovery → Coach) assumes users who search climbs are the same users who want form analysis. They may not be. Searchers optimize sessions. Analyzers want to break plateaus. Different pain states, different moments, different conversion triggers.
- Solo founder, bootstrapped: every hour on Discovery is an hour not spent making the AI coaching output *so good* that word-of-mouth does the distribution work for you. Discovery requires ongoing curation and community trust. Coaching requires technical depth — which is defensible.

**The actual constraint isn't focus dilution. It's identity dilution.** Two products don't just split your time — they split your positioning. What does Kilter-Up *mean* to a climber? If the answer requires a paragraph, you don't have a product, you have a portfolio. Portfolios are for funded companies.

**THE REAL QUESTION: What is the one transformation Kilter-Up delivers that no free tool can, and are you willing to go all-in on making that transformation undeniable before building anything else?**

### 🟢 Niche SaaS Founder

**Single product, two tracks — and sequence matters.**

Don't split. Two products means two brands, two GTM motions, two support queues. You have zero bandwidth for that as a solo bootstrapped founder.

But here's the real insight: **Discovery is your acquisition channel, Coach is your revenue engine.**

The V3-V7 climber opens the app to find a good 4x4 session. That's a daily habit — low friction, high frequency. They discover it through a friend's shared session link or a Reddit post. Discovery builds the habit loop. Coach monetizes the climbers who are actually obsessed enough to film themselves.

The conversion story writes itself: "Built you a perfect pyramidal session around slopers at 40° → here's what your technique looked like on your 4x4 attempt." That's not upsell, that's the natural next step.

**Pricing signal:** Free tier with Discovery (generous, viral), €7.99/month unlocks Coach. The free tier isn't a trial — it's a permanent product that creates community gravity. Paying for Coach feels like unlocking the premium layer of something they already love, not paying for something new.

**Community moat:** Your first 50 users define the vocabulary. Kilter obsessives have a specific culture — they talk in angles, hold types, grade pyramids. Make Discovery shareable ("Here's my 40° session") and Coach outputs screenshot-friendly. Community creates the content marketing for you.

**NICHE PLAYBOOK: Ship Discovery free in 3 weeks, let the Kilter subreddit and Discord spread it organically, then soft-launch Coach to the 50 most engaged users for feedback before charging anyone.**

---

### ⚫ The Executor

**Single product. Two tracks. Ship the funnel, not two codebases.**

The moat is the AI Coach — no competitor has it. But it has friction (filming). Discovery is the low-friction hook that fills the top of funnel and reduces churn ("stay for the sessions, upgrade for the coach").

Two separate products = two landing pages, two support queues, two billing setups, two positioning decisions. That's a week you don't have.

One product, two tracks means: one Stripe subscription, one domain, one auth system, one support inbox. Discovery is the free tier. Coach is the paid tier. Users discover climbs for free → hit a paywall when they want AI analysis → convert.

**MONDAY PLAN:**
- Day 1: Wire BoardLib search with grade + hold type filters
- Day 2: Build session builder endpoint: input goal (4x4, warmup, project) → AI returns ordered climb list
- Day 3: Frontend: Discovery page (search + filter + session builder). Minimal UI.
- Day 4: Auth gate: require login to save sessions. Capture emails.
- Day 5: Deploy. Share in r/climbharder, Kilter Board Discord, Instagram. Get 50 real users.
- Day 6-7: Read every signup's session. Fix top 3 UX blockers.
- Day 8: Add paywall: Coach tab locked behind €7.99/month Stripe.
- Day 9: Finish Phase 3c contextual analysis — context injected into Gemini prompt.
- Day 10: End-to-end Coach flow live.
- Day 11: Email the 50 Discovery users: "Coach is live — first month free."
- Day 12: Fix bugs. Add basic dashboard.
- Day 13: Write one honest launch post. Post everywhere.

## Chairman's Synthesis

### WHERE ADVISORS CONVERGE
All five reject the two-product split. Unanimous. Niche Founder, Executor, and SaaS Expert explicitly say "one product, two tracks." SaaS Expert and First Principles both identify that Discovery has zero willingness-to-pay because Climbdex is free — you cannot build a business competing with free on features. Niche Founder, Executor, and SaaS Expert converge on the same architecture: Discovery is the free acquisition layer, Coach is the paid monetization layer. This is the highest-confidence signal from the council.

### STRONGEST SINGLE INSIGHT
First Principles' reframe: "Friction that precedes transformation is called *commitment*." The entire two-product temptation stems from anxiety about video upload friction. But the friction IS the moat. Users who film themselves and upload are pre-qualified buyers — they're serious about improvement. Reducing friction by building Discovery doesn't reduce friction for the Coach; it builds a different audience that may never convert. The funnel assumption is unvalidated.

### BIGGEST BLIND SPOT
Nobody addressed **retention mechanics**. The Contrarian correctly flags that coaching is episodic (use → improve → churn), but no advisor proposed a solution. Discovery-as-free-tier partially addresses this (habit loop via session planning), but the group didn't design the actual retention mechanism: what brings a paying Coach user back week after week? Progress tracking? Session history? Grade progression charts? This gap will kill MRR if left unsolved.

### THE DECISION
One product. Discovery is the free tier (search + AI session builder). Coach is the paid tier (€9.99/month). Ship Discovery first to build audience, gate Coach behind Stripe. Do NOT build two brands, two codebases, two marketing motions.

### MONDAY MORNING ACTION
Ship a minimal Discovery page (search + session builder) on the existing Vercel domain within 2 weeks. Post it on r/kilterboard. Measure signups before writing a single line of Stripe code.
